
\textbf{\MakeUppercase{Abstract}}

Large Language Models (LLMs) have revolutionized the way we interact with artificial intelligence. However, along with these advantages, challenges also arise. Like any powerful technology, LLMs can also be misused; this is where Jailbreaking comes in (a set of techniques aimed at bypassing LLM security restrictions, causing them to respond to commands that would normally be blocked). The objective of this paper is to analyze how Jailbreaking has evolved from brute mathematical optimizations to techniques involving semantic stealth, human persuasion, structural manipulation, and API response pre-filling. The study is structured around an exploratory approach grounded in a literature review. Initially, the evolution toward semantic stealth and the use of psychological persuasion are presented (Sections 2 and 3). Next, language structure manipulation, response pre-filling control, and methodological procedures are addressed (Sections 4, 5, and 6). Finally, the study's conclusions are presented. The results indicate that combining pre-filling techniques with persuasion achieves success rates of up to 88\% to 89\%, highlighting the fragility of current alignments and the need for AI-based defenses.

\keywords{Large Language Models (LLMs). Jailbreak. AI Safety.}
