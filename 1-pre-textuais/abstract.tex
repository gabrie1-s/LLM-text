\textbf{\MakeUppercase{Abstract}}

Large language models (LLMs) are vulnerable to \textit{jailbreak} attacks, 
which seek to induce responses that violate their safety constraints. This 
article critically examines different approaches to these attacks, 
highlighting the relationships among token optimization, semantic 
stealth, structural transformations of language, persuasion, and response 
prefilling (\textit{prefill}). The study adopts an exploratory approach 
based on a literature review, discussing the mechanisms and experimental 
evidence reported in the selected studies. The analysis relates the methods 
according to the points at which they intervene in interactions with the 
model: input construction, argumentative framing, and control over the 
beginning of the output. The studies discussed indicate that these strategies 
exploit complementary dimensions of the attack surface and that certain 
combinations can increase their effectiveness. However, differences in 
models, evaluation datasets, and success criteria limit direct comparisons 
across results. The analysis concludes that security assessments should 
jointly consider the request, the context, and the conditions under which 
responses are generated, encompassing both model behavior and the features 
exposed by access interfaces.

\keywords{Large language models. Jailbreak. Artificial intelligence security.}
